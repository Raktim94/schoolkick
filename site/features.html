School attendance and family updates, without the clutter

What SchoolKick does today, in the order a school day happens.

Attendance for teachers

A teacher sees only their own classes. Opening one shows the roster with four statuses: present, absent, late and excused. “Mark unmarked as present” fills the usual case in one tap, then the teacher changes the exceptions and saves.

  • Marks are saved as events. A change to an existing mark needs a reason of at least three characters.
  • Unsaved marks are kept on the device, so a dropped connection does not lose the class.
  • Teachers can look at the full history for the day: who marked what, and when.
  • Dates in the future are refused. “Today” follows the school’s own time zone.

A family view that needs no app

A parent or guardian signs in from any browser. They see each linked child’s attendance for today, per class, and a two-week strip. If the school corrected an entry, it says so. A guardian cannot see a learner the school has not linked to them.

An overview for principals

The overview answers “what needs me now?” before it shows numbers: classes that have not taken attendance today, recent corrections, and learners with no guardian linked. Below that are today’s attendance, a trend for the last 7, 14 or 30 days, and a class-by-day register that shades each cell by attendance rate. Every chart has a table view.

Moving data in

Import learners from a CSV with the columns name, external_id and guardian_email. “Check file” lists every problem by row and column: duplicate student IDs, unknown guardian emails, names that are too short. Nothing is saved until the whole file is clean, and then it is saved as one step.

People, roles and the record

Roles are owner, registrar, teacher, finance and guardian, and a person can hold more than one. The owner sees an audit log of who created what and who corrected which mark. The log cannot be edited or deleted. Read how access is enforced.

Built to be fast and usable

The app is a single web page that installs to a phone’s home screen. It follows the system’s light or dark setting, works with a keyboard, respects reduced-motion settings and uses plain system fonts. It targets WCAG 2.2 AA. It has not yet had an independent accessibility audit.
